# Reaching Both Audiences — Strategy

**The situation:** your content speaks to the US healthcare insider. Your audience is
overwhelmingly the global career-changer. Evidence from the comment audit:

- India — *"persuing ba English and literature degree in India. can i take mha as my master's degree? ... I don't have any healthcare background"*
- Brazil — a practising dentist asking about switching into an MHA
- Hong Kong — *"working as a clerk in Hong Kong public hospital for 7 years, recently planning to immigrate to UK"*
- *"Does this apply to just US? how about aussie, UK Canada....."*

**The instinct is to pick one. You don't have to — but you do have to stop treating them as
the same viewer.** They want different things, they pay differently, and they need different
entry points.

---

## The two audiences are not a spectrum. They are two funnels.

| | **US Insider** | **Global Entrant** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Already works in US healthcare or is in a US program | Outside the US system, trying to get in |
| Core question | "How do I move up?" | "How do I get in at all?" |
| Buys | Interview prep, negotiation, career acceleration | Credential evaluation, pathway clarity, entry strategy |
| Price tolerance | High ($27–$297 proven) | Low individually, but 10–50× the volume |
| Volume | Smaller | Much larger |
| Where they are | US time zones, LinkedIn | Global, YouTube search, WhatsApp groups |

**Same channel can serve both.** What must change is the *signposting*, not the channel.

---

## Layer 1 — The universal content (already works, do more)

Some questions are the same on both sides of the border. These are your highest-leverage
videos because they serve everyone and rank globally:

1. **Degree choice** — MPA vs MHA, MHA vs MPH, MBA vs MHA. **60% of all questions.** Border-agnostic.
2. **Getting in with no experience** — the #2 question. Identical pain in Lagos and Los Angeles.
3. **What the job actually is** — *"What does a healthcare administrator actually do?"* Travels everywhere.

**These are the top of both funnels.** No targeting needed. Make more of them.

## Layer 2 — The split content (tag it clearly)

Each audience needs content the other doesn't. The rule: **put the audience in the title**,
so YouTube and the viewer both route correctly, and nobody feels baited.

**US Insider track** — titles name the US system explicitly:
- "How US Hospital Hiring Actually Works"
- "Administrative Fellowships in the US — The Real Fast Lane"
- "US Salary Benchmarks for Healthcare Administrators"
- "Negotiating Your First Director Offer"

**Global Entrant track** — titles name the barrier:
- "Can You Use an MHA From Abroad in the US?"
- "Credential Evaluation, Explained (for Healthcare Admin Roles)"
- "Working in a Public Hospital Outside the US — What Transfers"
- "The Honest Truth About Coming to US Healthcare From Another Country"

Do **not** bury this. A global viewer who clicks a US-salary video and gets visa content feels
misled; a US viewer who clicks global content and gets immigration talk feels it's not for them.
Titles are the routing layer.

## Layer 3 — Playlists as the segmentation mechanism

Create two playlists and feature them side by side on the channel home:

- **"Working in US Healthcare"**
- **"Coming From Outside the US"**

Then add a **pinned comment on every relevant video** pointing to the right playlist. This is
the cheapest possible personalisation and it works on every video you've already published.

---

## Mechanics that make this work

**On existing videos.** Do not re-record 253 videos. Instead:
- Pin a comment: *"US-based? start here → [playlist]. Outside the US? start here → [playlist]."*
- Add the playlist link to the description of your top 20 videos by traffic

**In the description template.** Add one line to every new video:
> *Outside the US? See the "Coming From Outside the US" playlist for the credential side.*

**In thumbnails.** Global-track videos get a globe or a passport motif. US-track videos don't.
Consistent visual grammar teaches your audience to self-select before they click.

**In titles, one constraint.** Never promise a US-specific outcome in a title without naming
the US. International viewers are your largest segment and the most easily alienated by
content that turns out not to apply to them.

---

## What NOT to do

- **Don't make a second channel yet.** You have 6,190 subs and one channel that hasn't been
  optimised. Splitting now halves both. Revisit at ~25K.
- **Don't translate yet.** Year two. Same songs, new audio — but only once the English
  catalogue is working.
- **Don't water down the US content to be globally palatable.** The US specifics are the
  moat. The fix is routing, not diluting.

---

## Monetisation split — this is the important part

The two audiences should **not** be sold the same thing.

**US Insider** — sell the existing stack:
- Interview Answer Vault $27
- Interview Coach $19 / $49
- Career Navigator $29/mo
- Career Accelerator $297

**Global Entrant** — the current products under-serve them. An interview prep product is
useless to someone who can't get an interview in that market. Serve them with:
- Content first (the global playlist) — this is a *lead* audience, not a buying one yet
- A low-ticket product: **"US Healthcare Admin Careers: The International Pathway"** — a
  $17–$27 guide covering credential evaluation, which degrees transfer, what employers
  actually check, and the realistic timeline
- Explicitly **do not** push the $297 Accelerator at them until they're US-adjacent

**Getting this wrong in either direction is expensive:** pitch the $297 to a Brazilian dentist
and you burn trust with your largest audience; ignore them entirely and you leave a large,
loyal, low-competition segment unserved.

---

## How to tell which audience a video actually reached

Your YouTube analytics will show geography per video. Build a simple monthly read:

1. Pull views-by-country for the last 30 days of uploads
2. Tag each video as universial / US-track / global-track
3. Check the match: a global-track video should skew international, a US-track one should skew US

**If a US-track video is pulling 60%+ international views, retitle it.** That mismatch means
the routing failed, and you are paying for it with early drop-off.
